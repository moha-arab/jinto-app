# Reyna — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned Reyna years ago, unlearn her: half the old heal, a shorter faster Dismiss, an overheal that expires, and an ult with no timer at all. The 30-second Empress doesn't exist.)*

## Who you are on the team

You are the most self-sufficient player in the game and the most selfish. No smokes, no recon, nothing a teammate can use; your whole kit is a reward printer that only pays on your kills. The deal your team makes when you lock her: you take first contact every round, and in exchange your entry kill doesn't get traded, because Dismiss deletes the window where they normally punish it. Win the first isolated duel, convert the kill into either escape or health, and take the next fight at full HP or better while every other duelist carries whatever the last one left him. That's the snowball, and it's the only thing you offer. A Reyna who isn't winning duels contributes nothing, the lowest floor of any duelist, so if you aren't willing to take the first fight of every round, play someone else.

## The kit, for real

**Soul Harvest**, the passive, is the whole agent. Anyone who dies within three seconds of taking your damage drops a Soul Orb, and you don't need the killing blow; chip damage before a teammate finishes him still pays you. The orb lasts three seconds, and Devour and Dismiss both eat from the same purchased pool: two charges, shared between heal and escape. Read that again, because it's the skill ceiling. Every kill starts a three-second clock on a three-way decision, heal, vanish, or save the charge, and the clock is short enough that starting to think after the kill is already too late. Decide before you peek what the kill will buy.

**Leer** is the only thing you own that works with zero kills. The eye deploys through walls, so you can put it behind the cover a defender is holding before you ever show yourself, and it nearsights every enemy looking at it for about a second and a half. That covers one swing, not five people walking through a choke; the team-execute Leer where everyone piles in behind it dies to the math. It only blinds the enemies facing it: someone watching a different angle sees fine, so it buys you the specific duel it's pointed at and nothing else. It's also destructible, and fragile enough that a couple of bullets kill it, which makes it an information tool running the other way. If the eye dies the instant it appears, someone is pre-aimed exactly where you were about to swing, and the correct play is to not take that peek. Throw it high and off to the side of where a crosshair naturally rests, so killing it costs a real flick, and throw it as your swing starts, not before; a Leer that pops early is a doorbell. And move it around: the placement they've seen twice gets shot on reflex.

**Devour** turns an orb into 50 health over about three seconds, up to 150 if you overheal, and the overheal drains away after ten seconds. Two things stop it being free. The channel is loud and visibly tethered to the orb, so healing anywhere an enemy can see or hear you is an invitation; they time the swing off the sound. And moving behind something that blocks the orb cuts the heal off mid-channel, so you heal near the orb behind hard cover or you don't heal. The ten-second decay writes its own rule: overheal only when you're about to take the next fight. Overhealing and then sitting passively on an angle is spending an orb on health that won't exist when the fight arrives.

**Dismiss** makes you untouchable for a second and a half, faster than you can run, and completely gunless: nothing hurts you and you can hurt nothing. Your gun goes away before it starts and comes back late after it ends, so finishing a Dismiss anywhere near an enemy is losing a gunfight by choice. And outside your ult you're not invisible during it, you're a bright purple ghost they can watch the whole way, which means everyone tracks where you went and greets you there. So Dismiss is never "away"; it's an escape to a specific piece of cover you chose before you peeked. Recast it to cut it short the moment you arrive. And it saves exactly one person. It does nothing for the teammate who walked in behind you.

**Empress** costs seven points and lasts until you die or the round ends, so there's no timer to feed anymore. The fire-rate stim is real but small; it isn't the point. The point is three upgrades to the loop: charges become unlimited, every kill heals you automatically and that health is permanent, no decay, and Dismiss turns fully invisible instead of purple. You reappear with an audible laugh, so it's not silent, but they lose the track. One second of windup means you pop it before contact, never in the middle of a fight you're already losing. It's a chain tool: worth it when three or more of them are alive to feed it, or banked for a retake where every kill re-heals you between duels.

## Attack

Take first contact. That sentence is the whole section; everything else serves it.

The execute: Leer through the wall onto the angle most likely holding the choke, timed so it lands as your team commits, swing with it, win the duel. Then the orb decision, and on entry kills the answer is almost always Dismiss. You just made noise in front of their whole team, the trade is coming, and surviving the entry is worth more than the health. Wait one beat first, long enough to see whether anyone is actually swinging, because Dismissing away from nobody wastes the charge and your position both.

Understand what your kill bought. Your "space" is not utility, it's the corpse. The moment you win the opening duel there's a hole in their setup, and it closes as fast as they rotate, so if your team isn't flooding in behind you the kill converted into nothing. Say it before the round: you're hitting first, they're coming with you. And ask who's smoking the choke, because nothing you carry blocks a sightline; hitting an unsmoked crossfire donates the exact duel you're supposed to win.

Money: the full kit, two Leers and two orb charges, is 900 credits and fits next to a rifle on any real buy. But the orb charges only pay if you kill someone. On a round where you die dry, that was money spent on nothing, which is one more reason the first duel has to be stacked in your favor, Leer up, team behind you, not a coin-flip peek.

Post-plant you turn back into a normal player, and this is where Reynas throw won rounds. You have nothing to slow a defuse and no reason to hunt. Sit in a crossfire with a teammate, hold your angle, let them walk into you. The snowball resumes when they peek.

## Defense

Your job is one pick and an exit. Take the forward angle or the off-angle nobody else on your team can afford, because for them getting found there is death and for you it's a Dismiss. The whole play is scripted before you peek: this angle, this fight, and if it goes wrong or the trade shows up, Dismiss to that cover, chosen in advance. Win the duel and leave anyway. A 5v4 with you alive, reset behind your team, maybe overhealed, is the round tilting your way; staying for a second kill in a position they now all know about is how the pick becomes a trade. The escape only exists if the path does: peek a spot where the Dismiss line runs through open ground and you've got a get-out-of-jail card with no door.

Empress banked is a retake plan by itself. Pop it before you enter, not after contact, and the retake becomes a chain: each kill re-heals you permanently and your Dismiss is invisible, so you win a duel, vanish, reappear somewhere they aren't watching, and take the next one at full health. It's the closest thing the game has to a fair 1v3, which is exactly why you don't spend it earlier on a round your team is already winning.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **The orb, every kill.** Trade coming now: Dismiss. Genuinely low, behind cover, nobody near: Devour. Healthy and safe: touch nothing; two charges have to last the round. The reflex press is the rank gap. The beat you wait to read the swing is the skill.
- **Dismiss needs a destination.** No cover picked before the cast, no cast. A Dismiss into open ground trades death now for death in a second and a half, gunless.
- **Heal quiet or not at all.** If they can hear the channel, they're timing the push off it. Behind terrain, near the orb, or skip it.
- **The dead eye rule.** Leer shot instantly means a pre-aimed enemy; cancel the peek. Finding that out was the eye's job, so don't throw the answer away by swinging anyway.
- **Empress before contact.** The windup plus the lost surprise means popping it mid-fight is paying seven points to panic. Three-plus alive with a chain in front of you, or a retake; otherwise it keeps.
- **Overheal is a countdown.** 150 HP means fight now. If the plan is to hold for the next half minute, the overheal won't be there when it matters, so don't buy it.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **The reflex orb.** Devour on every kill, or chasing the three-second timer forward into ground the kill didn't earn. The orb is a decision, not a pickup.
2. **Healing in a window.** Devouring where they can see or hear it. The fastest way to lose a round you'd already won.
3. **Panic Dismiss.** No destination, open ground, or straight toward them, and you re-materialize with no gun in your hands.
4. **Leer as decoration.** Head height where killing it is free, thrown before anyone's ready to swing, or the cardinal sin: eye instantly shot, peek taken anyway.
5. **Baiting.** Lurking behind teammates on the one agent whose entire value requires the first duel. A Reyna playing second contact is a four-and-a-half-man team.
6. **Ult-brain.** Taking fights you'd never take without Empress because Empress is on. It makes won fights chain; it doesn't make bad fights good.
7. **Trusting old overheal.** Positioning like you still have 150 long after the heal. Outside your ult it's gone in seconds; check the number before you take the aggressive angle.
