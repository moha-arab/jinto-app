# Sova — core

(written against patch 13.00, July 2026)

## Who you are on the team

You are the reason your duelist's fight is unfair. Every ability exists to make an enemy show himself or split his attention at the moment a teammate swings. A scan nobody swings on rounds to zero. The win condition: info in teammates' ears while it's still true, and you alive to keep producing it. One Immortal, reviewing his own climb, counted zero to two first deaths in 90 percent of his games and calls that the core of his Sova. A dead Sova costs the round twice: once on the hit, again on the retake that happens blind.

## The kit, for real

One fact covers the whole kit: every draw is a death window. The bow comes out slow and charges slower, gun away the whole time; Konpeki, a Radiant coach, calls the animation the kit's core weakness. Get behind cover, off the choke, before you pull, or a peeker catches you mid-charge and kills you for free.

**Recon Bolt.** Not a metal detector, a timing weapon. The counterplay is one bullet, so the dart's value is about when it lands. Fired into dead air, it gets broken for free and the breaker learns where you are. Landing as your team floods the choke, it forces the impossible choice: shoot it and give your entry a free swing, or hold the swing and get wallhacked. Learn the bounce before the lineup: banked off a wall, the dart lands around corners and through doorways, where breaking it means turning away from the fight or is impossible outright. The bank also lies about where you fired from, since the last leg points somewhere you aren't. What it never buys is stealth: the dart is loud and visible in flight on purpose, enemies are meant to hear it coming, so spend the bounce on a landing spot they can't punish, not on hoping nobody heard the arrow. Mains rate improvising a one-bounce through a door above fifty memorized darts. A clean scan is not a clear: the dart only reveals what's in its line of sight, and patient defenders wait out the pulses, so "no reveal" means unknown, never empty. And a broken dart is still intel: someone stood there to shoot it, and the sound of the shots says roughly where. Showliana says counting them tells you almost as much as a scan. Say the negative read out loud: "A main clean, they're mid or B" rotates your team.

**Owl Drone.** Worth money only if somebody can act on what it sees in the next few seconds; otherwise you paid to tell nobody anything. Fly it low and wall-hugging, crosshairs live at head height, and launch it close, not from Narnia, or half its life dies in transit. Fire the first marking dart immediately and you get a second per flight; most Sovas never use it. Only tag when the tag gets used, an Operator about to get swung or a target you're about to ult; the dart animation pauses the drone and gets it shot. You're defenseless while piloting, so fly from behind cover or a teammate. Dying mid-drone leaves your team blind and a man down at once.

**Shock Bolt.** Its money use is burning static utility. Shock the Cypher trips or Killjoy setup covering an entrance before the hit; a cheap dart just deleted the info net they paid real credits for. Almost nobody does it; one Immortal said he barely saw it across an entire climb. Second use, the pre-swing flush: fire at the common close-hold angle a beat before your duelist swings; even zero damage makes the defender shuffle, and the duelist wins the flinch. Third, the heard rush: shock the choke exit as they pour through. Running players have inaccurate guns and backpedal off the chip; even a graze starts their next duel down a chunk of health. What it is not: a gun. Lobbing shocks at someone currently shooting you loses the duel and the charges.

**Hunter's Fury.** The only good trigger is live information. Two of three blasts kill anybody, so a drone tag or a teammate's callout from two seconds ago makes it near-guaranteed; a blind fire is a light show that marks you. Pause between blasts, since everyone jukes after the first; Crashies clicks and drags while firing so the shot timing is unreadable. Move between shots, the beam traces back to you, and fire all three: the window is short, and expiring with a saved shot is a beginner signature. It kills Killjoy's Lockdown through the wall, but the price is all three blasts: two leave it standing. Track her ult and decide up front whether hers is worth the whole of yours. The X-cancel exists for one pattern, Konpeki's 1v2: dart the spike, ult to peel the defuser off, cancel to win the gunfight with the flanker, drone-tag whoever re-sticks. Canceling kills the ult on the spot and the unfired blasts go with it: you're trading leftover shots for a live fight, not banking them; the threat to the defuser was worth more than the damage.

## Attack

Default round: a recon inside the first ten seconds. Not hitting yet? Throw it mid: contested mid space your team walks behind beats a dart hoarded for an execute that may never come. The cooldown backs this: an opening dart recharges before the hit, while one burned casually mid-round leaves you empty when the team commits. When you're dartless, say so, and don't take the info-peeks the dart exists to take.

For the execute, reason from the defender: where does the anchor hide when this choke floods, where does the rotate arrive? Put the dart where that player has to be, timed to land as your team enters, and count it down. Crashies notes Zekken announces "dart lands in two" so teammates swing on the ping, not after digesting it; enemies reposition between pulses. Against a fast hit, invert the order: drone first, since the drone is slow and telegraphs, and keep the dart, which deploys instantly mid-swing. The layered version, what Crashies calls sky darts, standard in pro play: launch a slow high-arc dart, then drone. Defenders back off the drone into site, and the dart lands behind them, forcing the turn just as your team walks in. And the cross-site fake is free: defenders read a Sova dart as "execute here," so dart the site you're not hitting to buy a slow rotate.

The drone-entry pattern: your duelist walks a half-step behind the drone through the choke, and the defender shoots the drone and eats the swing, or hides and concedes the space. You enter last; Sova walking in first to use his own scan inverts the role. After the scan your job is the next call, not the next kill.

Post-plant: a dart at the spike's feet forces the defuser off or feeds spam. Shocks fired separately create two deny windows. The double-stack only works dead-center; a teammate planting a meter off your spot silently breaks it, and once enemies have seen it they bait the darts and defuse in the gap. Early rounds are the one honest window for doubles, since unshielded targets don't need perfect placement. Otherwise space them, and call your deny order so you don't shock a spike Viper already mollied and waste a full charge.

## Defense

Your body belongs at distance. The guns that pair with this kit are the range-and-wallbang ones, Operator, Ares, Odin, even a Sheriff, because a scanned enemy behind a thin wall is only a free kill if your bullet reaches them. Hold the long sightline where gun and scan overlap, and let a dart hold the second angle for you. You are not the close anchor: the kit takes seconds to draw and the retake runs on your scan, so falling back alive beats any 50/50 close fight. Just ration the dart-wallbang combo; run it every round and it's pre-broken by round four.

The biggest rank-up lever on this side comes from Darkovya's climb: stop autopiloting the round-start dart that tags two attackers who just shoot it and walk on. Save the recon for their execute, and as they flash and commit onto site, place it high on a wall behind them: scanned while committed, inside their own smoke, no free crosshair to break it. The round-start dart is fine while it works; when your early darts start dying without scanning anyone, that's the lobby where the saved dart wins. Build the habit early.

One good scan steers the round: how many, and is the spike with them; that decides rotate, stack, or hold. Hear a hit developing across the map? Drone the other site before your team over-rotates; over-rotating off sound is exactly what fakes are built to cause, and the drone is how you look at attacker-held space without peeking it with your face. On retakes, flashes and your dart go in together or the retake dies in the doorway, so announce your utility state constantly. If your dart is down or got broken, drone-clear the close corners before anyone swings; a broken dart means you know less, not more.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

Lineups: three-ish reliable darts per site that you rotate, not a library. A dart that fully clears a few angles beats one that half-scans many, because partial scans get teammates killed clearing "cleared" corners. One Radiant hit the rank knowing two lineups on one map. The AverageJonas library is a hobby, not the entry bar; treating it as the bar is why people quit the agent.

Drone economy: if you bought it, use it. Skipping the buy on true ecos is fine, but hoarding a bought drone for the right moment is the most common error Konpeki flags in student VODs.

Darts follow your teammates' fights, not your pre-round script: contact breaks out B while you default A, re-dart toward B.

Ult timing: live info only, and pick targets whose dodge is already dead: a defuser locked to the spike, a choke they're mid-funnel through, a fresh drone tag on someone committed. Distance is a coin flip either way, so quit choosing shots by range and choose them by denied dodge space. A teammate's live callout counts as a trigger; you don't have to be the one who saw them.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by how often they decide games:

1. **Dying with the bow out.** Charging a dart in the open or on the choke gifts a kill that erases both the hit and the retake. Cover first, then draw.
2. **Silent Sova.** Scans and broken-dart reads that never hit voice are utility spent on nobody. Your kit is comms with an arrow attached.
3. **Same dart, same timing, every round.** From round three your signature generates zero info and hands them your position.
4. **Lineup Larry.** Fifteen seconds aligning a pixel dart away from your team is a 4v5 you volunteered for.
5. **Droning when nobody's ready,** after the entry happened or before anyone's set.
6. **Wide-swinging a clean scan.** Defenders wait out the pulses. That corner is unknown, not clear.
7. **Forgetting shocks exist.** Dying with both charges while the trip net you could've broken for pocket change still stands, or panic-lobbing them mid-duel.
8. **Ulting a stale ping.** Three quick shots down one line get sidestepped, and the beam draws them a map to your head.
