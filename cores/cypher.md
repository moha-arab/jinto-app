# Cypher — core

(Written against patch 13.00, July 2026. 13.00 cut the trapwire windup from 0.9s to 0.7s: less time to break a tripped wire before the reveal. The kit was rebuilt in 11.08, October 2025: trips slow and reveal now, no concuss, and the cam warns enemies near it.)

## Who you are on the team

You're the tax collector. Every route through your part of the map costs the enemy time, utility, a pulled crosshair, or a body. Delay a hit ten seconds while three teammates rotate and you've done more than the Cypher who got a pick and died. Teams pick you over a fourth fragger because a webbed site half-holds itself: everyone else leans to the other site while your wires buy retake time. Your win condition is making entry two problems at once, the web and your gun from an angle the web doesn't advertise. Merge them, standing on your own trip, and you're a slow duelist.

## The kit, for real

**Trapwire.** Two wires, two jobs, and mixing them up is the number one setup error. Kill wires go deep, past the point of commitment: back-site, tight corridors, above all plant zones, since the one defenseless window in a round is the plant animation, hands on spike, gun away. A mid-plant trigger catches a body that can't shoot back, though the wire can still be broken on the walk-in. Info wires go on flanks and entries; a destroyed info wire already paid. They stopped, turned, shot it, and told you the flank is live.

Read the silence too: a flank wire quiet deep into the round means no lurker, so the missing bodies are on site or saving for the retake. Say that count out loud.

Put the wire where they can't clear it while moving forward, so breaking it forces a stop or a turn mid-entry: they die to your swing or lose all momentum. Just past the choke, never in it; a doorway wire gets shot from safety, a wire three steps deeper fires after they've committed to a room they can't leave clean. Stretch it full length and hide both anchor posts; a visible emitter is a free break.

Rotate heights so nobody sweeps one plane all half. Crouch-head is standard: unjumpable, uncrouchable. Floor wires pull head-level crosshairs down, and that look-down is your swing window, but crawling utility (Gekko's pets, Fade's prowlers, a boombot) clears them free; read the comp. Standing height, or a wire on the landing spot, catches Jett and Raze dashing to elevation at barrier drop.

**Cyber Cage.** The cage has four jobs and "be a smoke" isn't one of them: audio tripwire, isolation wall that turns a 3v1 into three 1v1s, delay, escape hatch. Bread-and-butter is cage on wire: the instant the wire triggers or gets shot, pop it, and they're slowed and blind while their team can't see in to trade. The pass-through sound is a spray timer; hear a body enter, shoot the smoke. Never pop on first sight, wait until they're at the center. And learn the hollow-cage reset: site collapsing, pop a cage, walk inside it, reposition. Nobody clears the inside of your own smoke.

One-ways: the smoke centers on the emitter, so an emitter on an elevation edge leaves a gap under the bottom rim. You see feet, they see nothing. The principle survives map updates, specific lineups don't, so re-test yours; a botched one-way blinds you while they walk in clean.

**Spycam.** The rework made the cam fragile: enemies get warned near it even while parked [VERIFY the 11.08 reveal and audio ranges], and a broken one is gone for the better part of a minute [VERIFY the 60s destroy cooldown]. Place it where they can't casually walk near. Tap in, snapshot, tap out; read the red pings off the minimap, since in cam view you're blind and audible. Don't dart every sighting; silent info walking into your team's crossfire beats a tagged enemy hunting the cam.

Default is the retake cam: parked high, untouched until your team comes back, then pinging exact positions into the re-entry. Discount wallhacks. The barrier-drop cam buys one snapshot then dies: right on pistol or a rush read, wrong as a plan.

**Neural Theft.** Expensive, and it works off any teammate's kill, not just yours. Retakes, executes into a suspected stack, man-down rounds where you must pick a site. Two pings, then gone; have the plan before pressing. Burning it on a random kill nobody follows up is throwing.

## Attack

Job one: the flank wire goes down the moment your team commits to a path, not when you reach site; placed after they already rotated, it's a monument to a lost round. Deep, where the flank routes converge, because shallow wires get jumped and a flanker reaches an angle on your teammates before one fires. Pick guaranteed activation over clever: a wire they're forced to shoot confirms the flank is live, one they never touch confirms nothing. Then rejoin; babysitting an idle trip is a 4v5 by choice. Re-place it when the team rotates. On multi-flank maps, both wires go to flanks and the cam watches the spike.

Early, throw the cam ahead into main: even if it dies instantly it peeked the angle your duelist was about to dry-peek, and an aggro defender it spots is a free opening pick. [VERIFY: sources don't cover wiring taken map control, like sealing mid behind you; confirm before teaching.]

In the hit, slot in behind the duelists and stay alive: there is no combat Cypher, and your payout arrives after the plant, which a dead Cypher never places. Your cage patches the controller's gap, the one crossing lane their smokes always leave open. If the smokes are spent when the spike comes out, your cage covers the plant.

The lurk has one legal start time: after your team makes contact somewhere else, because rotators then cross your lane not looking at you. Lurking from round start gets you found and alerts the whole enemy team. Never keep lurking while your team dies on site, and skip it on gun-advantage rounds; eco defenders stack and camp, turning your clean 1v1 into a 1v3.

Post-plant is your power spike: the defense kit flips around the spike. Wire across the retake path or over the plant, cage masking the wire so the defuser can't find it, cam on the door they must use, one cage saved. The pass-through cue says a body is on the spike inside your smoke. Spray it.

## Defense

Round start isn't dead time. Against rushers and on pistols, a wire right at the entry choke gets hit before they have sightlines, turning the rush into a pileup with a count attached. Against slow utility-heavy comps, do the opposite: web deep or placed late behind a cage, because setups standing at barrier are what darts and nades are calibrated to sweep. The same read picks the cam: barrier-drop snapshot against rushes, parked retake cam otherwise.

Then anchor. Play behind your utility, holding the angle it doesn't cover, and let the trap trigger before you peek; peek early and an information-advantaged fight becomes a coin flip. After a trigger, move: decent players pre-aim, and will wallbang, the spot the setup advertises. You're allowed to fight, too: the web lets you hold an aggro off-angle other sentinels can't, since a cage behind you covers the retreat and the wires punish the chase. Patient is the default, but a Cypher who never contests space teaches attackers they can clear at leisure.

Vary the web: run the same setup twice and a coordinated team breaks it with one dart. Change heights, swap the kill-wire side, place late after their utility wave.

Leave your site only on double confirmation: three-plus confirmed at the other site AND your own site quiet; one footstep or one broken wire is exactly what a fake looks like. Take the cam and untriggered wires if there's time; kit guarding an empty site is dead money, though a standing web still delays late re-rotates, so don't sprint back for it under fire [VERIFY: do trips stay armed after you die? Sources conflict; pre-rework they persisted after a 2022 buff].

Retake is why you saved the cam: re-place it high before the team re-enters and you're handing out wallhacks. Cages section the site, smoking the strongest defender-turned-attacker overwatch angle so the retake clears slice by slice instead of eating a crossfire; never cage your own team's re-entry sightline. If the site falls before the retake gathers, back off, group, go together. The solo re-peek costs a body plus whatever's left of your net.

The 1vX defuse is the kit's filthiest mode and most Cyphers never use it. Cage the spike and defuse inside your own smoke: they push in blind, and the pass-through sound says when one commits, so you know when to stop tapping and fight. A leftover wire goes across the path behind you first. If they've seen your cam, feed it to them; people reflexively shoot a spotted camera, and that crosshair is not on your swing.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

Util out or gun out. The actual climbing skill. Passive web-watching farms low elo, where nobody clears utility and nobody trades; above that, the default flips to taking fights around your utility.

Cage discipline. Everything live at the moment they commit; cages burned early get waited out, and the hit lands with your kit down. One cage stays pocketed all round, both sides: attack it denies the defuse, defense it's the smoke you defuse behind when your controller is dead.

Economy. The kit is cheap; buy it in full on save rounds, and pick untriggered wires back up at round end. One flank wire plus a pistol is a real pistol-round buy.

Setups. The player this community keeps pointing at, nAts [VERIFY still the reference Cypher in 2026], runs pedestrian setups and wins on decisions. Learn where people actually walk on each map and the most obvious wire keeps catching them.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

1. Same setup every round. By round four one utility sweep kills the web before it produces any info.
2. Cam burned in the first twenty seconds, or dying mid-feed-stare. You traded the retake wallhack for one rotation ping.
3. Flank wire placed after reaching site. The rotation it existed to catch already happened behind you.
4. Hesitating on the trigger. The reveal is short; waiting for more (the old concuss reflex) means someone breaks the wire first. Fight when the outline shows.
5. Blaming the coin toss. Set up A, they hit B, you retook with nothing. The fix is a pocketed cage and a cam covering the site you're not on, not harder anchoring.
6. Pure rat play. The web buys you better fights; you still have to take them.
