# Phoenix — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned Phoenix years ago, unlearn him: the flash is the free signature now, the fire is the cheap buy, the wall casts from behind cover, and the heal follows you out of the flames. The old kit doesn't exist.)*

## Who you are on the team

You are the duelist who needs nobody. Your own flash, your own vision cut, your own heal, and once the ult is up, a free second life: the whole job is taking space without spending a single piece of your team's utility. Jett takes space and leaves; you take space and keep it. The loop that wins rounds: flash in, win the fight for a piece of map, touch your fire, heal back toward full while you hold what you took, and shoot the first person who re-peeks into ground you now own. Other duelists get one committed fight a round; you're built to heal up and take the next one. Ending rounds alive but with the kit unspent means you played a worse rifle, not a duelist.

## The kit, for real

**Heating Up**, the passive, rewires everything else: touching your own fire heals you, up to 50 HP per ability, and the heal keeps ticking after you step out of the flames. So you don't stand in fire; you brush it and go, and the health arrives while you reposition. Hot Hands is the fast heal, a quick touch banks it; Blaze is a slow drip you collect while the wall does its actual job. The fire heals you alone. Teammates burn in it, so every molly and wall gets called out loud, same as a flash.

**Curveball** is the signature and the engine: one free charge every round, another earned back every two kills, a second buyable on real rounds. It flies a fixed curve, left-click bends left, right-click bends right, then pops and blinds every player with eyes on it, your team and you included. Two rules keep it honest. It's a corner tool: wrapped around cover it pops in their face; thrown across an open sightline it's a slow glowing warning they simply turn away from. And stand off the corner, as deep as the curve allows: there's over half a second between the throw and the pop, and the sound tells them to look away, so distance is what makes the pop beat their reaction. Your gun is back in your hands almost the instant you release, so the swing comes with the pop, not a beat after. Never peek early; blinding yourself on your own entry is the most Phoenix death there is.

**Blaze** is a vision cut that happens to heal you, in that order. Fire it and a wall of flame rises along the path; hold the button to bend it where you look. The cast travels through solid cover, so you can stand in complete safety and draw an 8-second wall onto the site: a bonus half-smoke your controller never paid for, from a Phoenix who never showed his head to place it. The wall's job is cutting a sightline so the fight on your side of it happens in a 1v1 world. It chips anyone who pushes through, and the drip heal is a bonus while it stands, but if healing or covering one quick peek is the reason you cast it, you wasted the closest thing you have to a smoke.

**Hot Hands** costs 200 credits and is the cheapest health in the game: a fireball that bursts into a pool of fire for 4 seconds, melts anyone who stands their ground in it, and pays you the 50 HP heal instead. The overhand throw lands at a consistent distance every time, about the edge of your run-sound ring on the minimap, so choke stalls and defuse denial need zero lineups. Order matters: it's a clear or a stall first, and you touch the flames after the fight to bank the heal on the move. Mollying your own feet before you commit is backwards; the heal is the reward for winning the fight, not the warm-up for taking it. Buy it every real buy round, skip it on ecos, where the free flash keeps you dangerous for nothing.

**Run It Back** is a 7-point second life on a leash. Mark your spot; for the next 10 seconds you fight, and whether you die or the timer runs out, you dissipate and return to the marker with full HP, guns reloaded, and armor only up to whatever you wore at the cast. Three mechanics decide everything. The timer always brings you back, mid-clutch, mid-win, doesn't care, so you cannot ult and then lurk. The cast is loud and the fade-out is visible, so an obvious marker gets camped and you revive into a pre-aimed crosshair. And killing your ult body puts nothing on their scoreboard: your first life is a fight they spend bullets on and can't even collect the kill for.

## Attack

The standard entry is three abilities back to back: Hot Hands onto the spot a defender most likely holds, Curveball wrapped around the entry corner, and you walk in on the pop while Blaze cuts the one angle the flash can't reach. You don't have to guess right everywhere; the molly moves him, the flash blinds him, and the wall makes everyone else's help arrive late. The greedier, better version: wall off the far half of the site entirely and take the near half like a 1v1, because behind an 8-second wall it is one.

Two flash throws are worth drilling. Back to a wall, throw so it pops behind and above you while you face away: you eat zero blind and everyone looking at you eats the full 1.5 seconds. Or pop it perpendicular to your entry path, off to one side, so defenders turn toward the light and away from your gun. Either way the full blind is short, so the swing is instant, and either way it gets called first, because your flash blinds teammates exactly as hard as enemies.

Win the entry fight and the round hinges on what you do next, and the wrong answer is leave. You have no escape ability. Backing out of won space just schedules a second entry later, without the utility. Touch your fire, hold the ground, and let them re-peek a healed Phoenix on an angle you chose.

Wall for your planter: eight seconds of flame over the open sightline turns a scary plant into a free one. Post-plant, Hot Hands is defuse denial with no homework; the consistent throw distance means you can stall the spike from positions they haven't cleared.

On ecos your threat never turns off: the free Curveball makes a pistol entry look like a buy-round entry, and two kills print the next charge.

## Defense

You're a delayer and a retaker before you're an anchor. Hot Hands into the choke stops a rush cold for 4 seconds and heals you while you give ground; Blaze across the entry cuts their sightline while the team rotates. What the kit will not do is get you out of a site collapsing on you, because there's no escape button. So take your delay, fall back alive, and win the round on the retake, where you're the best first man in the game: you flash the site for everyone, and with ult up you're the one player who can afford to swing it twice.

Early aggression is your other paycheck. Curveball the angle before you peek it and the peek isn't a gamble; a flashed attacker is a free swing, and even a whiff sends the rotate call early. But your self-sufficiency whispers that every fight is safe, and the round you die first, your team retakes 4v5 without its flash. Peek unpredictably, not constantly.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Throw the free flash every round.** Ecos included. It refreshes next round and regenerates off kills; a hoarded Curveball protects nothing and switches off the one threat you carry for free.
- **Ult at the start, not the end.** A clutch with Run It Back is just one player fighting twice. The same ult opening the round buys an angle you'd never dry-push: marker somewhere safe they won't guess, utility dumped into the push, and even the version where you die comes home with their setup mapped for the execute. You also return with the armor you wore at cast, one more reason it belongs early, not at the desperate end. Ten seconds, back regardless, so no lurk plans.
- **Heal after, not before.** The pre-fight comfort molly spends your stall tool to buy a heal you haven't earned, and the fight that mattered gets fought without it.
- **Corner or keep it.** Nothing for the Curveball to wrap around, no throw; wall the sightline or take the fight straight.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **Flash malpractice.** Open-sightline Curveballs they turn away from, throws hugging the corner so the pop loses to their reaction, blinded teammates who never got a call, and peeking before your own pop. One ability, most of his lost rounds.
2. **The clutch piggy bank.** Sitting on Run It Back for a 1v3 that never comes. Fighting twice in a clutch is still just you; fighting twice at the start of a round bends the whole round.
3. **The obvious marker.** Casting flat against the nearest wall. They heard the cast, watched you dissolve, and now they're pre-aiming the one spot you're guaranteed to appear in.
4. **Escape-duelist brain.** Kill, then back out reaching for an escape that isn't on the kit. Won space you leave is space you pay for twice.
5. **Blaze as a heal lamp.** Casting the wall to stand in it, or as cover for a single peek. It's an 8-second sightline cut, and it's your only one.
6. **Skipping the Hot Hands buy.** 200 credits for a 50 HP swing every entry turns lost trades into won ones; the players who skip it on buys are the same ones wasting it as a comfort heal when they don't.
7. **Cooking your teammates.** Your fire heals exactly one person on the server. A teammate following you through your wall arrives at the fight already hurt. Call your fire.
8. **Scheduled aggression on defense.** The kit makes solo peeks feel safe, so it becomes every round, then a read, then a 4v5. Aggression they can't predict wins rounds; aggression they can set a watch by loses them.
