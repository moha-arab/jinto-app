# Clove — core

*(written against patch 13.00, July 2026)*

## Who you are on the team

Riot gave the best ability in the game, smokes, to an agent with a duelist's engine: a heal that arms off kills, an ult that un-kills you, a smoke that casts from the grave. That's why Clove keeps topping ranked winrate charts no matter how many times they nerf her: Omen has the higher ceiling on a coordinated team, but his kit converts through teammates and ranked doesn't supply them. The engine only runs near fights, so your job is second body in: smoke for the team, take the trade fight behind the entry, die where the corpse keeps working. The win condition: every fight your team takes happens under your smokes, including the ones after you're dead.

## The kit, for real

**Ruse (smokes).** Two facts run your game: they recharge, and they're short. Near the shortest duration of any controller, with about 60 meters of cast range: you can't cross-map a smoke, but from mid that reach covers both sites' chokes on most maps. Recharge kills the usual rationing rule. Smoke early and often: a smoke spent at 1:40 is already recharging and soaks enemy util; hoarding was the confessed leak in the famous Immortal-Reyna-main winless-on-Clove thread. Recharge also makes you the game's cheapest faker: burn smokes on a loud A look, walk off, and the real B hit goes out on recharged charges. A fake costs you thirty seconds of patience, nothing more. Placement: hug the smoke against THEIR entrance to the choke, so attackers walk its full depth and pop out at one predictable point you can pre-aim; place it closer to your own side and they exit anywhere along a wide cone instead.

Post-death casting is the signature, and it got gutted: one charge while dead, a six-second smoke, placeable only within about 40 meters of your corpse. A dead smoke covers one moment (half a defuse, one retake cross, a one-way on the defuser), so where you die is a smoke lineup. Die at the choke your team has to fight through; a wide lurk death deletes half the agent.

**Not Dead Yet (ult).** Not a free second life: you come back, but you die again unless you secure a kill or assist before the timer runs out. So a good ult happens where a fight already exists and a teammate can convert. Covered, it wins both ways: enemies turn to shoot the ghost and your teammate kills them mid-turn, or they run and hand over the space. Nobody calls a Killjoy ult that catches no one wasted. The solo panic ult is the throw: re-form in the open, weapon-equip delay, nobody tags your killer, dead twice.

You come back with your gun and your smoke charges, so res-then-smoke is standard: place full-duration living smokes before you die again. Regen shield is the only shield that survives the revive: light and heavy die with you, but regen kicks back in after the ult and works you toward 125 effective HP. Hence the ult-round buy: Shorty (out-equips a rifle after re-form, and res fights are doorknob range) plus regen shield. Re-form behind cover or inside your own smoke, and spend the invulnerable window walking toward a teammate, so the chaser hunting the cancel-kill swings into a trade.

**Pick-Me-Up (heal).** It arms off a kill or a damaging assist, so tagging an enemy a teammate finishes still fuels the engine. The value is the next fight. Insta-popping after every kill is the top Clove habit at every rank: overheal and speed evaporate before anyone re-peeks. Hold it while unpressured and tap it the second before you swing; the speed boost only lasts three seconds, so popping it while tagged burns the whole window crawling. The cast is audible to enemies, and Diamond-plus players pre-hold wide for the swing they know is coming, so mix in narrow peeks off the speed. The overheal is 50 now, not the 100 you might have heard.

**Meddle (decay).** Least round-losing part of the kit, so briefly: it forces a decision. The holder either stops swinging while decayed and your team moves, or swings anyway and loses to body shots. Decide the follow-up swing before you throw; the window is short, and Plat-plus players just back off and heal. Lob it around a corner and swing on activation (decay needs line of sight to the fragment, so nothing bleeds back through the wall to you), throw it through your own smoke with a spray behind it, or put it on the spike to rip defusers off. The radius is tight; lazy lobs clip nobody.

## Attack

Default round: move with the pack, smoke the crossfire angles shooting into your approach, and enter second. Your duelist takes contact, you kill whoever trades them. Never first: die before any smoke exists and four teammates hit full defender vision while your corpse lands out of grave-smoke range. Never last or lurking: a deep-lurk death strands the corpse smoke over nothing, with nobody near your ult.

The short duration makes timing the discipline: don't smoke while the team is still clearing main, or the execute happens through thin air. Good defenders won't push your smoke either. They count it down and re-clear as it drops, which kills slow hits, so the re-smoke call is yours: you know the recharge state, and the team crosses on the fresh cast. If the hit keeps stalling, that's what the fake is for.

The engine prices your duels. A 50/50 inside trade range is subsidized: win and Pick-Me-Up pays you into the next fight; lose and you're res bait or a corpse that still smokes the site. The duel you refuse is the Reyna duel, the untradeable off-angle where no dismiss saves you and your corpse reaches nothing. Holding ult widens the license: take first contact on the hit; worst case, you come back as an extra body mid-fight.

Are you the only controller? In ranked you usually are, and then dying at 1:30 doesn't cost a body, it costs every smoke for the rest of the round minus one six-second grave cast. Solo, take your fight after the entry smokes are down and the site fight has started: trade range, not the opening pick. With a second controller, feed earlier; someone can still paint the map.

Post-plant is where the kit peaks, full stop: no other controller banks a smoke that keeps working after they're dead. Bank one smoke for the defuser, always: smoke on the spike forces a blind defuse while your team sprays a known pixel. Phantom over Vandal is a real decision here: no tracers through your own smoke, so the spray never prints your position. Dead post-plant, the banked grave cast covers the half-defuse window; say the six seconds out loud, a smoke nobody plays off is nothing.

## Defense

Default to mid, alive and passive: on most maps it's the one spot your smokes reach both sites' chokes, with your sentinel anchoring where you aren't. On the biggest maps, check the reach first; where mid can't serve both, anchor a site like anyone else. Some strong Cloves play aggro mid for the flank pick, death priced in, grave smoke covering the entries anyway. The death pricing is wrong even though the spot is right: six seconds of corpse smoke doesn't buy back a body fed at 1:30, and you're mid for the living reach to both sites' chokes, an edge the site-anchoring Clove gives up. Hold it boring. Peek off mid only with a read and a fast exit; the position pays in smokes, not picks.

Re-smoke chaining is your defensive identity: back-to-back charges wall a choke for close to half a minute, and the third cast lags about a dozen seconds behind, which is exactly the window a counted-down push targets. Don't smoke first footsteps; place it when they're close enough that it splits the entry from the bodies behind and blocks the trade. Against flash-heavy comps, set the choke smoke deeper into their tunnel so the flasher exits on a known path you can pre-fire and wallbang. While alive on a choke, build the one-way: smoke high enough that you see feet and they see nothing; Ascent B's version is the green boxes held from Boathouse.

Dead in the opening seconds with a site left weak? Spend the grave cast on the likeliest route in: six seconds of wall plus the re-clear it forces often bounces attackers into your stack. You get one cast: pick the scarier route. In a 4v5 with attackers committed to your site, don't reflex-smoke main. It can shove them to your weak side and turn a winnable site fight into a losing retake; you need an equalizer, so smoke to isolate: cut one attacker off from their teammates and hand your site player a clean 1v1. Alive at plant time? Stop smoking. Save both charges, announce the retake, sequence them: first smoke walks the group in, second goes on the spike mid-fight so someone can stick or fake the defuse against enemies peeking blind. Spend both early and the retake goes in dry, the most common dead-Clove throw in ranked. Dead at plant time, your one cast goes on the spike.

Defensive ults: never take the long-range one. Attackers back off, the timer expires, you re-die for nothing. Make them commit into a choke or onto site first, where disengaging costs the round.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

The ult is round equity. Even her own designer says it can be correct to ult with no expected kill if the round matters enough for the game state or economy. Body-blocking ten seconds of a 5v4 on an eco-breaking round is a real play. Hoarding it costs whole uses: at eight points it charges back well within a half, so the ult you bank to the buzzer is one you never fired. Die first in a winnable round? Res and go; the classic miss is the Clove who plays their life, dies last, and banks it forever. Two hard nos: a chaser inside ten meters with no teammate to trade, and ulting to stop a defuse you can't reach because teammates are pinging you. The wasted-Sage-ult trap; ignore the pings.

An unspent ult on your corpse is pressure by itself: enemies babysit the body or risk a res behind them. Play the babysitter, don't feed them. Res when a teammate can trade, because the cancel-kill hunter walks into the swing; alone, leave it unpressed. The threat already slowed their round, and a res just refunds the kill.

Buys: pistol is Pick-Me-Up, smoke, light shield; the heal is disproportionate on pistols. Ult rounds add Shorty and regen shield. Phantom over Vandal, as covered.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **Playing Reyna with smokes.** No dismiss turns Reyna's off-angles into untradeable deaths while your team eats every crossfire unsmoked.
2. **Not smoking at all.** The single biggest complaint from Clove's own teammates. If your smokes-per-round sits near your kills-per-round, you're the stereotype.
3. **Dying where your corpse reaches nothing.** Wide deaths delete the grave smoke and the ult conversion in one stroke.
4. **Solo panic ults.** Re-forming in the open, long-range defensive ults, nobody to trade. Dying twice, at a premium.
5. **Hoarding recharging smokes.** Clove is the one controller where stinginess is strictly wrong.
6. **Insta-popping Pick-Me-Up**, then re-fighting at base speed with faded HP.
7. **Dead-smoke reflexes.** Panic-smoking main in a 4v5, or spending everything before the retake starts.
8. **Playing the old Clove.** Long grave smokes, multi-charge dead casting, 100 overheal: she doesn't do any of that anymore.
