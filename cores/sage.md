# Sage — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned Sage years ago, relearn the numbers: the wall is cheaper and dies faster, the self-heal is fully back, and the res reaches further than you think.)*

## Who you are on the team

You are not the healer. You're the team's clock: wall and slow decide when fights happen and where, the heal decides who's at full health when they do, and once every two or three rounds the ult un-kills somebody and flips the math. You're also the only player on the server who can place terrain: cover that didn't exist, a plant that can't be shot, a boost onto an angle nobody holds. Play her as a sentinel who happens to heal, never a medic who happens to own a wall; wall placement swings more rounds than your healing ever will.

## The kit, for real

**Barrier Orb** buys seconds, not the round. It's cheaper than you remember and softer too: each segment starts weak and hardens to full strength about two seconds after it lands, and a team that commits gunfire chews through it fast. It's still the best 300 credits in the game because of what those seconds contain: a plant, a defuse, a rotate, a res. You can cast it from fifteen meters, so stop standing in the choke to place it; drop it from cover and stay alive. The whole wall expires forty seconds after it goes up, all segments at once, which is the timing rule everything else hangs on: a wall placed on a guess at round start is gone when the real push arrives. It survives you dying. One thing it cannot do: the spike shatters any segment it touches, so you can't wall the spike away from a plant or a defuse; wall the sightlines to it instead. And you can ride it up as it rises: a boost onto an angle nobody pre-aims is a real weapon, the first time.

**Slow Orb** is 200 credits of time and information. Seven seconds where everyone inside moves at half speed, dashes at half speed, and can barely jump; and everyone in it crunches audibly, even walk-stepping, so it doubles as a tripwire that tells you the moment they commit. The field covers a standard chokepoint. Two rules make it worth the money. It slows everybody, you and your team included: a slow on your own retake path or your own feet is griefing yourself for 200 credits. And a slow nobody shoots into is a donation: half-speed enemies in the open are free hits, so throw it where a teammate's rifle, an Operator, or damage util is already pointed, or hold it.

**Healing Orb** is the recovery layer, not the identity. It's free, it comes back every forty-five seconds, and it restores a full hundred health over about five seconds to any damaged teammate you can see. The self-heal is the same full hundred now, not the scraps you might remember, so patching yourself up after an aggressive play is a plan, not a last resort. Two failure modes. Healing mid-firefight: the heal pauses every time the target takes damage, so you stand there exposed accomplishing nothing; heal in the pocket between fights, aimed at whoever takes the next duel. And hoarding: a heal sitting off cooldown all game healed nobody. Stinginess with a free forty-five-second ability is the quiet version of not buying util.

**Resurrection** is seven points and roughly three seconds of you doing nothing else. You can start it from up to twelve meters away, which means from behind cover far more often than the old habit of standing on the corpse; use that. The body is immune while it's encased, but your ally comes back with a hundred health, no shields, and a beat of stand-up animation where they can already be shot, and you're defenseless the entire time. So: secure first, res second. Wall the body, smoke it, or know the area is clear; then take the first safe man-advantage res you're offered instead of saving the ult for a movie. A revive that turns a retake defense from 3v4 into 4v4 wins the round on the spot; a hoarded ult in a lost round healed nothing.

## Attack

You go through the choke second or third, never first. Your opening duel is worth less than your wall, and dead Sages place no walls.

The wall has three jobs on attack; pick one per round. Cut the sightline the anchor or the retake will shoot your planter from, so the spike goes down: the classic, and still the highest-value wall in the game. Block one half of a crossfire so your entry fights one angle instead of two. Or boost a teammate onto an off-angle before the hit. Because it casts at range, you can wall for the plant without ever standing where the anchor is aiming.

Slows on attack are flank insurance or post-plant money. One on the rotate path punishes the fast flank and crunches out the info even when nobody's watching it; hold the second for post-plant, on the defuse or the retake path, where seven half-speed seconds under your team's crossfire usually is the round.

After site is yours and before the retake lands, heal the entry. The duelist sitting at 40 is about to take the next opening fight, and the heal is the difference between him trading and him being a gift. Top yourself off after; you at full health is one more real gun.

Res on attack is strongest after the plant. Drag the fight to a body behind your wall or a smoke, revive, and the retake walks into an even fight plus a ticking spike. Reviving during the execute itself, into an uncleared site, hands them the same kill twice.

## Defense

Anchor a site, or hold the middle where you can react toward either half. Your kit stalls pushes, so it lives where pushes happen.

The wall's timer drives everything here. Do not wall at barrier drop on a guess: forty seconds later the real hit arrives and your 300 credits are a puddle on the floor. Wait for the sound, the util, the call that says they've committed, then wall; it hardens fast enough that a wall dropped on contact reaches full strength before they're through it. Place it deeper than the entrance, not flush against it: a wall on the lip gets broken for free from safety, a wall deeper in has to be broken while your team shoots the people breaking it, and that trade is the product you're selling. And vary it. The same wall three rounds running gets pre-shot and walked through, and a wall that blocks your own teammate's peek or retreat is worse than no wall.

First slow goes on the choke the moment they commit: half-speed attackers stacked in a doorway under guns is the best value the ability has. Keep the second charge for the plant attempt or your own retreat off the anchor spot, and remember it slows you too.

Between waves, heal the chip damage, yours and the other site player's. The side whose anchors are at 100 when the second push comes wins the second push.

On retakes you set the tempo. Wall one post-plant angle out of the fight so your team fights two players instead of three, or wall off the deadliest angle onto the spike so whoever sticks the defuse fights one gun, not the whole site. Beside the spike, never on it; the spike shatters what it touches, remember. Res on retakes only behind cover or cleared space; a res into a live post-plant crossfire is one ability that kills two of you.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Wall now or wall later.** The question is never "could a fight happen here," it's "has it started." Committed push: wall. Noise: hold. The forty-second timer forgives nothing.
- **Which gun does the slow feed.** Before you throw it, name the rifle, Operator, or damage util that punishes it. No gun, no throw. The crunch alone is worth it only on flanks, where the info is the point.
- **Who gets the heal.** Whoever takes the next fight, in the window nobody's shooting them. Not the healthiest player standing nearby, not the guy at 95, never anybody mid-duel.
- **Pistol wall.** On pistols those 300 credits are your armor or your gun upgrade; buy the wall only when you know the exact fight it wins, not as a habit.
- **Res on eco.** The standard line is skip it: a revived pistol dies again and those points belonged to a buy round. The honest answer is judgment; a genuinely safe res that makes it five bodies holding sites can be right even broke. What's never right is a risky res on a round you were losing anyway.
- **The combo is the kit.** Wall plus slow funnels them into one predictable path. Wall plus res is the standard safe revive. Slow plus anything that shoots is a kill zone. Any ability spent alone should make you ask what the second one was doing.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **Utility at the first sound.** Wall and slow dumped on a footstep fake, nothing left for the real hit. The most common Sage throw at every rank.
2. **Playing medic.** Backline Sage, wall in pocket, waiting for someone to need healing. The wall shapes rounds; the heal recovers them. That priority order never flips.
3. **The decayed or pre-broken wall.** Placed at barrier drop and dead before the push, or placed identically every round until they shoot it down on arrival. Its cousin: walling a teammate out of his own peek.
4. **Res misuse, both directions.** Blind-reviving an exposed body into a held angle so two of you die, and sitting on seven points all half waiting for perfection. The first safe man-advantage was the perfect one.
5. **Mid-fight heals.** It pauses on every bullet they take; you're exposed and healing zero. Between fights or not at all.
6. **Self-slows.** Your own retake path, your own feet, your own defuse area. Half speed for seven seconds, self-inflicted.
7. **The third wall boost.** The boosted off-angle kills once, maybe twice. After that you're a stationary head on a pedestal they aim at before they round the corner.
