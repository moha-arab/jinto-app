# Jett — core

*(written against patch 13.00, July 2026. If you learned Jett years ago, unlearn her: one updraft, short smokes, a dash you have to earn. The old version doesn't exist.)*

## Who you are on the team

You are the crosshair magnet, not the fragger. On attack you go in first so five defenders look at you while your team walks out of the choke and shoots them in the ear. If you die in your smoke and the site falls, you did your job. On defense you're paid aggression: dash makes early info peeks refundable and erases the Operator's weakness, the exposed beat after a shot. Pro play drifts on and off her with the meta; in ranked she's a permanent top pick, because ranked defenders punish dashes far worse than pros do.

## The kit, for real

**Tailwind** is the entire rank gap. Iron Jetts press E after they get shot. Radiant Jetts press E before a fight they've already decided to take: the committed peek, not every routine look (defense runs a different rule, below). There's a windup before the dash is armed, so a panic press means the windup runs while you're eating bullets, and you die with dash "active." Arm it behind cover instead, escape one button away. The catch: the armed window is 7.5 seconds, and if it expires the charge is gone until you kill two people. Arm when a fight is imminent; one activation buys one planned fight, not two.

**Cloudburst** lasts 2.5 seconds. That is not a controller smoke, it's a starting pistol: the moment it lands, you move. On attack it blocks the specific angles defenders can shoot you from as you commit, placed so you land beside it, not inside it. A smoke you dash into the middle of is a body bag; they spray it until they find you. Don't spend them on rotations; if both are gone at execute time, you smoked nothing that mattered.

**Updraft** is one charge, which makes it a commitment: spend it offensively and you have no vertical escape for the rest of the round. It's also loud. Use it to get above the head-height slice defenders actually pre-aim, to clear Cypher trip height on entry, and as Blade Storm's other half. The same boost spot works about twice a game; after that it's a pre-aimed free kill. That's the whole ability.

**Drift**, the glide, is two mechanics depending on what's in your hands. With knives it's the point of the ult: gliding slows your fall while the knives stay perfectly accurate, buying aim time from above the band their crosshair lives in. With a rifle it's death in slow motion: your gun sprays wild in the air, theirs doesn't, and a floating Jett is a free head for anyone who looks up. The classic updraft-then-hang death. Gun out, get down fast; glide to eat fall damage and to knife, nothing else.

**Blade Storm** is an economy weapon most players use as a panic button. Left-click knives are perfectly accurate while running, jumping, and gliding, and a kill refreshes the full set. Right-click dumps every remaining knife and refreshes nothing. So: move constantly while ulted (standing still hands the rifle back its advantage), fight from the air, and treat right-click as point-blank desperation. Your knives are not an 800-credit Sheriff, they're an 8-point ult: up close, flick and commit several; at long range, single deliberate taps. Knife kills count toward the two-kill dash recharge, so a knife eco snowballs: knife one, take his rifle, second kill re-arms your dash.

## Attack

Start with their Operator: nothing in the game answers one as cleanly as an armed dash. The Op's weakness is the beat between shots, and your dash is faster than it. Show him something cheap, a shoulder or a jump-peek, and make him commit the shot, then dash across or at him while he rechambers: an Op needs well over a second between shots and your dash lands in under half of one. You don't need the kill; an Op that's been dashed at stops holding the angle honestly, and that hesitation is the sightline your team walks through. Never try it with dash un-armed.

Your buy is a rifle; an Operator on attack fights your job description. Pistol round you're still the entry: Ghost plus a Cloudburst, and respect the Classic: past 20 meters it's a coin flip, two-tap and strafe out. Won pistol: Bulldog if full armor fits, Stinger or Spectre if not.

Pre-round, get the smoke plan out of your controller's mouth; a ranked Jett cannot entry without a smoke setup, and nobody arranges it unless you do. Same with initiators: the flash you never asked for is why the dash died.

First up the choke, jump-peek with dash armed; a tagged jump-peek any other duelist has to eat is one you can refund, because an armed dash still fires the instant you press it, tagged or not. It tells you if the angle's held and if the next smoke is even needed. Then the rule that decides most of your deaths: do not exit dry. Smokes down, initiator util committed, at minimum somebody's flash. Nothing and nobody? Walk in with the team.

The dash itself: arm in cover; pick the exact landing spot before you press (no planned spot, no dash; smoke and swing instead); hold a movement key, since holding nothing dashes you straight forward; land where an enemy must expose himself to your teammates to shoot you. While flying, sweep your camera across the close corners: anyone your screen touches gets pinged on the minimap, so even a death-dash buys information, and the sweep stops you dying to the close player you flew past. Exit the smoke from a side, never the front, never instantly. One geometry test governs everything: if two angles can shoot your landing spot, don't dash there; your escape beats one crosshair, not two.

After the opening kill, get one and get out; the signature amateur death is pushing for a second kill into the refrag. Fall back into the space you made, let the team collapse, then take a deep position before the plant; rotators path predictably. Once your area is clear, setting up post-plant beats re-clearing site for slow teammates.

Mid-entry feels wrong? Second Cloudburst at your feet and re-swing a different edge so they re-guess your exit.

Never lurk on attack: shortest smokes in the game, zero support tools. A lurking Jett is a baiter with extra steps.

## Defense

This is where the Operator lives. Dash deletes the sniper's weakness: an armed Tailwind makes it a fight where they risk everything and you risk nothing. Never scope in with dash un-armed; hold long sightlines only (short angles get run-and-gunned by SMGs); one shot, hit or miss, then dash out and set up somewhere new. Off-angles belong to you like nobody else: getting found in one is normally death, for you it's a dash. Take the spot nobody pre-aims, not the default pixel everyone jiggle-checks, and never the same spot two rounds running. After a pick, run the Op toward the other site: their instinct is to rotate away, and you beat them to their own rotate.

Plan the Op like a win condition: Sheriff on defense pistol for the long angles, then off a won pistol, Outlaw round two, Op out by round four. Lost pistol, don't chain force-buys to rush it.

Your smokes have defensive jobs nobody uses. Curve one around a corner to one-way their Op's angle without ever standing in it, landed high enough that only legs show. And when your dash is spent and someone's re-peeking you, look at your feet and smoke yourself: 2.5 seconds is nothing on a rotation and an eternity in a re-peek.

Rifle rounds, do the thing only you get paid for: fight a deep aggressive angle at round start. If they answer with util you dash out alive, and that early call lets the team stack four the other way with full rotate time. But don't pre-arm dash for routine looks; take the first look dry and save the arm for when they're actually running you down.

Retakes: drop the Op for a rifle; it holds pushes, it doesn't retake sites. Don't dash onto site into the post-plant crossfire: attackers sit two-plus angles on every entrance and the geometry test fails almost every time. Updraft is the better retake key; re-entering at a height nobody holds turns a set crossfire into two people flicking upward. Your smokes are worth roughly nothing here; 2.5 seconds buys one swing, never a defuse.

Concussed, you dash at half speed, and slow fields like Sage's cut it the same; the cover you planned around may be out of reach, so back off.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Arm or don't.** Is a fight coming inside the window? Yes: arm now, in cover. Maybe: keep it.
- **Dash or smoke-and-swing.** No pre-planned landing spot, no dash. Ever.
- **Fight or save.** You're the best gun-saver in the game: arm dash, break one sightline, and the Vandal (or the Op you lifted) comes home to swing the next buy. Calling the round dead one fight early is worth more than most of your kills.
- **Knives and updraft go together; dash stays in reserve.** Grounded with knives out, you're standing at the head height they're already aiming at. But don't dash-updraft INTO a two-man site: you kill one and the second trades you with your escape spent. Updraft in, fight from the air, keep the dash to relocate mid-air for the second duel.
- **Ult economy.** Knives on save rounds, drop your gun to a teammate, still buy heavy shield. Sitting on an 8-point ult for five rounds "for a clutch" is throwing in slow motion.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **Reactive E.** Shot first, dash second, dead during the windup.
2. **Dry entry.** Dashing before smokes or util, nobody positioned to trade. A 4v5 with the space-taking tool spent.
3. **Arming on a hunch.** Window expires, charge gone, real fight twenty seconds later with no escape.
4. **Overheating.** Opening kill, then a greedy push into the refrag instead of banking the space.
5. **Fighting the lost round.** Dying rifle-in-hand in a 1v4 instead of saving. Everyone else has an excuse; you have a dash.
6. **Panic right-click.** Ult over, one knife of value extracted. Its cousin: standing still while ulted.
7. **Op greed.** Re-peeking the same angle, scoping with dash down, holding short angles, refusing to drop it on retakes.
8. **Mobility-as-invincibility.** Taking fights you shouldn't because you can "just dash out." Works until they pre-aim the exit.
9. **Floating.** Rifle out, spacebar held. A gliding Jett is a stationary head for anyone who looks up.
10. **Never walking.** Dash and updraft are loud; heard twice, defenders are set before you arrive.
