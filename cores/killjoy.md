# Killjoy — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned Killjoy as "drop the gadgets and leave," unlearn her: the current kit only works with you standing behind it.)*

## Who you are on the team

You're the Sentinel who makes one area of the map expensive to enter without ever needing to win the duel yourself. Your currency is time and information: the deployables delay pushes, announce exactly where people are, and punish hasty site hits, which lets your team play four strong somewhere else. The turret costs nothing, so you have intel every round, broke or rich. And you own the hardest spike denial in the game: hidden swarms end defuses, and Lockdown makes touching the spike physically impossible for eight seconds. One rule sits above everything: the turret and the bot run on a leash of about forty meters to your body. Walk out of it and they silently switch off; walk back and they take two loud, visible seconds to wake up. The swarms answer to a different rule: they don't care where you stand, only that you're alive. Either way the gadgets don't hold the site. You hold it, from behind them.

## The kit, for real

**Turret** is eyes first, chip second, a killing tool never. The bullets do single digits, but each one slaps a heavy slow on whoever it hits, so one burst turns a runner into a slow walking target and tells you exactly where the fight is. It sees a 100-degree cone, not the 180 you might remember, which means it gets one job: pick the single question you need answered, "is anyone coming from here," and aim the cone at exactly that; the preview when you place it shows what it covers. Hide it off the obvious sightline or above head height so it lands a full burst before someone spins and shoots it; a turret in plain view dies before it speaks. It's free, so it goes down every round, ecos included. When you rotate, recall it and it's back in twenty seconds; let it get shot instead and you're blind for a full minute. And don't point it somewhere a smoke will predictably land. A smoked turret is furniture.

**Alarmbot** is your Vulnerable button, not a damage tool. It sits invisible until someone steps within a few meters, then chases them down and detonates, and for four seconds they take double damage from everything: your gun, your swarm, your turret. Placement is one idea: around the corner past where they pre-aim, so it triggers point-blank and there's no time to shoot it. It has 20 HP; in open ground it gets spotted, dies to a single bullet, and did nothing. On attack it moonlights as a flank alarm. Same leash, same recall rules as the turret.

**Nanoswarm** is your round currency: two charges, and every plan you make spends them somewhere. Thrown, it goes near-invisible on the ground; set off by hand, it grinds 45 a second for five seconds across a circle wide enough to swallow the spike and force anyone off the defuse. Two things decide whether it ever earns its money. Hiding: corners, props, edges, never open floor, because the entire ability is that they don't know where it is. And the trigger: it takes about a second from press to full burn, so activate on confirmation, turret fire, the bot's bang, the plant or defuse sound, never on hope. You can't pick it back up mid-round, it cooks teammates too, and if you die your swarms go dark with you.

**Lockdown** ends rounds. Thirteen seconds of windup, then everyone it catches in a radius that covers a full site with room to spare is detained for eight: crawl speed, guns holstered, no abilities, no planting, no defusing. Your own team is untouched and fights inside it freely. The enemy's options are exactly two, and you're choosing between them for them: break the 200 HP device during the windup, or abandon the space entirely. Breaking it works through walls with damage utility, a Sova dart, a KAY/O molly, so a wall alone isn't a hiding spot against every comp, but you're alerted the instant it dies. Placed right, behind cover relative to where they are with the radius still swallowing the ground that matters, both options cost them the round. Call the timing out loud; eight seconds of unarmed enemies matters exactly as much as the guns pointed at them, so teammates should be swinging as the detain lands.

## Attack

Attack is your weak half; play it as a specialist, not a fifth rifle. The job is flank lock, then post-plant.

Turret and bot go behind the push, watching the path a flanker takes. That releases a teammate from watching it, which is real value every round. The leash still applies: place them along the route at a spot you'll stay within reach of, not back at spawn, or they switch off the moment the team moves up.

Both swarms are for after the plant, and this is the discipline that decides your attacking rounds: a swarm spent stalling one defender before the plant is a defuse you can't deny after it. Post-plant, hide them where they cook a defuser, take a crossfire angle off the spike, and fire them on the defuse sound, not on footsteps. A defender who starts the tap inside your swarm leaves it or dies in it, and either way your clock keeps running.

Lockdown on attack is a site-take button. Deploy it protected before the hit: defenders either leave the site for the windup plus eight or burn utility breaking it, and both ways your team walks on nearly free. The even better round is taking the site without it, because post-plant with Lockdown still in your pocket is the closest thing this game has to a round already won; their retake ends when you press it.

If you're walking out first with the duelists, you're playing someone else's agent. Enter late, keep your body and your util alive, and be there for the phase of the round only you can play.

## Defense

This is your half. Anchor a site and make it defend itself well enough that your team can afford to be strong elsewhere.

Setup logic that works anywhere: turret watches the widest, longest entry line, where the burst plus slow buys the most seconds. Bot covers what the cone can't see, or sits where an entry trips it mid-commit. Swarms hide at the choke or on the ground people actually plant on. Then the piece lazy Killjoys skip: you, alive, on a quiet off-angle inside the leash, playing behind the delay. The bot's bang is your cue to fight, because Vulnerable doubles your bullets and doubles the swarm you buried it in, and nothing walks through both. The layered version, bot sitting inside a hidden swarm with the turret watching the pair, kills people who made no mistake.

Half your value is your mouth. Turret fire on the far side of the map is a rotate call your team gets seconds before any other comp would; say it every time, even when it feels obvious.

When the real hit comes and you're down bodies, don't die on the site. Fall back with your gun and retake behind Lockdown. That's the other reason you exist: a retake with Lockdown up is nearly unloseable, since for eight seconds the attackers can't hold their angles, can't use utility, and can't touch the spike while your team walks back in shooting. A Killjoy who dies on a lost site gave away the delay and the retake in one death.

And vary the setup: depth, side, aggression, every few rounds. Spots that repeat get pre-fired, and a pre-cleared Killjoy is four abilities of nothing.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Hold the leash or rotate for real.** Half-rotations are poison: drift toward a fight and the turret and bot silently switch off behind you. Stay connected, or recall and commit to the rotate.
- **Activate on confirmation, never on hope.** The swarm needs a beat to reach full burn; fired at an imagined sound it reveals itself, burns nobody, and your stall is one charge thinner.
- **Stall now or deny the spike later.** Ask what the last twenty seconds of the round look like. If a defuse or a plant is coming, the early stall isn't worth it; let them walk through the bot and your crosshair instead.
- **Lockdown on a commit, not a guess.** Nine points does nothing to an empty site. Fire it on a confirmed hit or a retake you're genuinely taking, teammates set to swing on the detain, and never "to stall" when a 200-credit swarm stalls fine.
- **Stay alive for the post-plant.** Your death switches off everything you placed. In a bad fight, saving your body and your swarms wins more rounds than the hero peek; count that cost every time you consider one.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **Predictable setups.** Same turret pixel, same swarm corner, round after round. By the third time they pre-fire your whole kit and hit a naked site for free. This is the Killjoy sin.
2. **Wasted Lockdown.** Dropped in the open and broken for nothing, fired with no one positioned to follow it, used on a guess, or hoarded until the match ends. Nine points buys a round; spend it like one.
3. **Breaking the leash.** Chasing a kill or rotating without recalling, silently switching the turret and bot off; the walk back doesn't fix it, because they wake up loud with a two-second tell enemies can play around.
4. **Utility in the open.** The bot and the swarms die to a single bullet once seen. Visible placement converts 200 credits into a sound cue.
5. **Stacking everything on one square meter.** Both swarms overlapping, bot hugging turret: one grenade, one careful clear, and the round's whole investment is gone. Spread the coverage.
6. **Playing the gun instead of the clock.** Ego peeks, dying before the execute even arrives, both swarms spent pre-plant with nothing left for the defuse. A live Killjoy behind placed util is a delay machine; a dead one is a site handed over with the receipts.
