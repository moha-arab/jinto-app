# Raze — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned Raze years ago, unlearn her: satchels arm slowly and everyone hears them, the nade no longer shreds utility, and the bot dies in a blink.)*

## Who you are on the team

Every ability you own deals damage; no other duelist can say that. So your job is not to win aim duels, it's to make positions unlivable. A defender anchored in a close corner has three options against you: eat an explosion, step into your crosshair, or leave, and all three are wins. You take space with explosives where other duelists take it with their bodies, and satchels put you on angles nobody pre-aims. One thing to get straight about the job: nothing in this kit says attack-only. Damage that breaks a rush is worth as much as damage that starts one, and nobody delays a hit onto a site like you. Coach yourself for both halves.

## The kit, for real

**Boom Bot** is a pressure tool wearing a scout costume. It rolls in a straight line, bounces off walls, and locks onto the first enemy in its cone, chasing until it explodes for 30 to 80 depending how close it catches them. That almost never kills a healthy enemy; what it does is force a decision. Whoever it finds picks a poison: shoot it and confess a position, ready for your swing; eat the blast and take the next duel hurt; or leave the angle and hand it to you. It lives five seconds, not the ten old guides taught, and gunfire kills it faster than you remember, so it clears the one specific corner you're about to use, not the whole site. Roll it down the path you intend to walk in the next five seconds and peek beside it, so the defender aims at the robot or at you but not both. One charge per round; a bot rolled out at nothing for "free recon" is a round where you face-check the corner yourself.

**Blast Pack**: two charges, sticks where it lands, detonates on re-use or on its own after about five seconds. This is your movement and your identity, and it has one rule people refuse to learn: it takes a second and a half to arm, and before that it only moves whatever it hits. Thrown at the guy pushing you, it shoves him without a scratch. Armed, it does up to 50, and it never hurts you or a teammate, so launch off your own feet all day. The shove isn't a consolation prize either: bump a defender off his angle or up out of cover and physics did the aiming for your team. For movement, satchel at your feet, detonate, fly; a second pack thrown mid-air is the double satchel and it covers ground nothing else covers. But hear this: enemies hear your first satchel from a long way out, so every satchel entry is announced. And a satchel they shoot just dies, no blast, no shove; one placed where someone's already looking is 200 credits of nothing. A single you land clean beats a double you fumble into the open.

**Paint Shells** is free and comes back every two kills, which is the whole strategy: spend it every round. Throw for distance or lob it short; it bursts, then splits into cluster charges that burst again, up to 55 apiece. The catch is the falloff: catch someone at the edge and it does nearly nothing, and it no longer chews up enemy utility the way you might remember. So it isn't a "damage in that direction" button, it's a "you can't stand there" button, and it only works on places they can't leave: a tight corner, a doorway they're committed to, the cubby your teammate's recon just lit up. Into open ground they take two steps and watch it paint the floor. The best version: nade the position, then satchel in as it bursts, so whoever moved off it meets you mid-panic.

**Showstopper**: 8 points, one rocket, 150 dead center falling to a chip at the fringe. It explodes on contact with anything, the wall beside you included, and it kicks you backward when you fire; in a tight corridor you kill yourself with your own highlight. The equip sound is famous, everyone hears it and scatters, and there's a windup before the rocket leaves, so pulled standing in the open it's a light show. It doesn't stay out forever either, roughly ten seconds, so pull it when the shot exists, not in case one appears. Fired from an off-angle or the top of a satchel jump, the sound points at the wrong place and their reaction time evaporates. And the rocket doesn't have to kill: five defenders forced off the spike, or a retake that stops at the door, is a kill's worth of round with zero bodies.

## Attack

Clear space before your body enters it; that sentence is the whole playbook. Boom Bot down the main path first: whoever's holding either shoots it and tells your team where they are, or gives up the angle. Paint Shells the corner defenders actually hold on that site. Then, and only then, the body: satchel in behind your initiator's flash with a teammate close enough to trade. Bot, nade, flash, body, in that order. Run it backwards and you're a duelist who announced herself with a satchel and arrived alone.

Timing is the discipline. The moment your first satchel pops, their whole team knows Raze is coming and roughly where. That's fine when the site is being hit by five people and the noise is one thread of many. It's fatal when you're early: the announced entry with no follow-up is the most predictable death in your book. Team not ready? Don't satchel. Walk in with them and save the packs for the fight on site.

The entry itself: pick your landing spot before you leave the ground, and pick it by whose crosshairs can reach it. You're loud and airborne, so surprise isn't what you get; what you get is an arrival angle nobody holds and speed that beats their flick. Land beside cover, not center stage, and land where a defender who turns to shoot you shows his back to your team. Keep the second pack when you can: landed with a satchel in pocket you're still mobile, with both spent you're a slow duelist holding a nade.

Post-plant is where the kit prints rounds. A lobbed Paint Shells stops a defuse from complete safety, and a held Showstopper turns their five-man retake into a question of who wants to die grouped. Alive after the plant with nade or ult in hand, you're worth more than your gun.

Buy both Blast Packs every round you can afford them. A one-satchel Raze isn't mobile, she's a grenadier with a good jacket.

## Defense

You hold with damage. Early round, roll the bot or throw the nade into the path they like rushing: a five-man sprint that eats a cluster grenade at the choke isn't a sprint anymore, and the bot's chase tells you the rush is real while your team still has time to answer. Momentum broken and info gained, for utility you should never die holding.

Satchels on defense are two things. First, off-angles: fly to the elevation nobody checks and hold it with a Judge or Shorty, because the fight you've created starts inside shotgun range, faster than their aim adjusts. Second, the exit: take your pick, then satchel out the instant it lands. The re-peek after a first kill is where aggressive defenders donate rounds back; you don't have to re-peek, you're already gone.

Showstopper on defense is a retake and anti-rush tool, fired off teammate info into the choke as they commit or into the post-plant cluster. Don't burn it winning a duel a Vandal wins.

And don't rot in the backline. Parked deep behind the fight, you're four damage abilities and a flying body doing nothing; every round your explosives touch no one is a wasted pick. You're not the deep flank either, your kit is loud and its value is at the fight. Live close to where the damage will be needed.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Bot at a target, or bot in pocket.** It clears one specific place you're about to use. No specific place, no throw.
- **Armed or just moved.** For its first second and a half a satchel only shoves. Point-blank on a pusher that's a shove, so pair it with your gun or your teammates; never expect it to finish alone.
- **Single or double.** Double for distance you genuinely need, single for fights. Trading the fancy jump you hit half the time for the plain one you hit every time is worth more than any trick.
- **Nade now or nade on info.** It's free and comes back every two kills, so spending it every round is right; spending it into open ground is not. The bar: can they leave the area before it bursts?
- **Ult or gun.** One isolated enemy: gun. A cluster, a retake, a defuse to deny: rocket, from an off-angle or mid-jump, never standing in the open, never with a wall at your nose.
- **Loud entry or quiet walk.** The satchel announces you. Team set to trade: fly. Team lagging: walk with them and be loud later.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **The solo announced entry.** Satcheling onto site ahead of everyone with no trade in position. They heard you coming, you arrived alone, and the space you took is re-taken over your body. Includes rolling the bot or throwing the nade without a word, so the enemies it moves get shot by no one.
2. **Ult theater.** Equipping in the open so everyone scatters, spending 8 points on one guy a Sheriff kills, or firing into close geometry and headlining your own highlight.
3. **Paint Shells into nothing.** Open ground, no info, dodged in two steps. Its quiet cousin: hoarding the free ability that two kills bring back.
4. **Boom Bot as a scout.** Rolled out at nothing, or followed blindly into the person killing it.
5. **The forced double.** Combat doubles you can't land, dumped short into the open, when the boring single was the play. And forgetting they heard satchel one: a flash timed at your landing spot is the standard counter.
6. **Backline Raze.** Passivity wastes this kit like no other; four damage abilities and a body that can fly should never finish a round unspent.
