# Chamber — core

*(written against patch 13.05, September 2026. If you learned him in the two-anchor days, unlearn it: one anchor, an 18-meter leash, and a trap that watches the flank from across the map again.)*

## Who you are on the team

You're the sentinel who holds ground with a gun instead of a gadget wall. Other sentinels lock a site with utility and can be elsewhere while it works; nothing in your kit stops a push by itself, so the lock is you, personally, rifle up, every round. The win condition: make one angle dangerous enough that attackers have to spend utility just to make you leave, take the first pick, teleport out before the trade arrives, and set up again somewhere they haven't cleared. Do that once and you've cost them time, utility, and a player for one bullet. You also bend your team's economy in a way nobody else can: an ult that's a five-shot sniper nobody paid for, and a pistol that one-taps heads at any range for a hundred credits a bullet. Chamber teams stay lethal on the rounds every other team is just trying to survive.

## The kit, for real

**Rendezvous** is the agent. Free, one anchor on the ground; while you're inside its 18 meters (walls and floors don't break the leash) you press it again and you're standing on the anchor just over a second later, gun already up. It's not a movement tool, it's life insurance you buy before the peek: the whole agent is "take the duel you're allowed to lose." Three facts run every decision you'll make with it. The trip starts with a shootable windup, so a teleport pressed after you're already getting hit is a death animation. The anchor has 50 HP, everyone can see it, and broken means gone for the round, no comeback; a visible anchor gets cleared by a couple of stray bullets or a grenade from someone who breaks anchors on principle, and then you're a sentinel with no kit. And the teleport is loud: everyone in the area hears it, so it buys you distance, never secrecy. After a teleport or a recall you wait half a minute; during buy phase it resets almost instantly, so moving it between rounds is free.

**Trademark** is a 200-credit flank alarm that doubles as a kill assist. It works from anywhere on the map; walking away doesn't switch it off, so the old habit of babysitting it is dead. When it spots an enemy it counts down briefly, then slows everyone in the area to half speed for four seconds, dashes included, your teammates included. It's paper: 20 HP, visible, one bullet ends it. It's also information both ways: broken means someone came through, silence means the route is clear. The catch that decides placement: it dies when you die and shuts off if you're suppressed, so its information is only worth what your life is.

**Headhunter** is the best eco weapon in the game and a mediocre everything else. A hundred credits a bullet, eight max, leftovers carry to next round. The headshot kills a full-armor player at any distance, no falloff, which nothing else at that price can say. A body kill on that same armored player takes three bullets and three hundred credits, so this is a head-clicking tool or it's a scam. Spread opens up after the second fast shot: aim down sights, tap, pause, tap. Never spam it.

**Tour de Force** is a five-shot sniper for 8 ult points, no refills. Any hit from the waist up kills; a leg shot leaves an armored player alive and angry, so never take the shot that only shows you feet. It fires slowly enough that a miss means you relocate, not re-peek. And every kill drops a slow field where the body fell: kill the first man through the choke and everyone behind him wades in at half speed, the closest thing this game has to a stop-pushing button.

## Attack

You're second contact, not entry. Let the duelist take the opening fight and be the gun that kills whoever kills him; a Chamber who walks in first spent the team's insurance on a coin flip. Trademark's default job on attack is the flank route behind the team, deep on the route and behind cover, so a lurker trips it a corridor before he reaches anyone and spends four seconds crawling through the slow. Trap plus anchor makes you the flank response all by yourself: the trap tells you he's there and pins him at half speed, and a half-speed enemy in a known spot is a free swing. The other use is keeping it in your pocket and throwing it at the one corner you'd otherwise have to clear on the way in; if it starts counting, someone's home.

Lurking, which most players do as disguised baiting, is genuinely yours: anchor behind you, one aggressive look at their rotation path. Any other lurker who guesses wrong dies alone in a corner; you're back at your anchor before the trade arrives.

Post-plant is your best phase. One long line that punishes the defuse path, anchor behind hard cover, and a simple trigger: shoot the first player who crosses, and the moment utility comes at you, leave. You're not there to win the retake fight; you're there to make touching the spike cost a life or a utility dump, twice if they're slow about it.

Spend the ult on rounds where one opening pick decides everything, usually the rounds your team is poor: a top-shelf sniper on a round only the other team paid for. Eco rounds are Headhunter rounds: light shield, bullets, and the longest sightline you can find, because the ranges where every other eco gun gives up are exactly where yours doesn't.

## Defense

The loop, every round, in order: anchor first, behind cover, somewhere that can't be sprayed from the attackers' side of the map; Trademark on the second way in or the flank; then push up to an aggressive first-contact angle and take the opening duel. One shot, then out as contact builds. Reset onto a different angle or fall back into the crossfire with your team, and change elevation when the position allows it: even the enemy who guessed where you went has his crosshair at the wrong height.

Then the rule that keeps you alive past round three: rotate the setup. The same off-angle with the same anchor lasts exactly as long as it takes them to pre-aim the angle and pre-fire the landing spot. You're not hiding what Chamber does; everyone knows what Chamber does. You're hiding where.

Don't teleport as a rotation shortcut. The leash barely moves you anyway, and the thirty seconds after are exactly when a push arrives. Recall is the rotate tool: the moment you know the hit is elsewhere, pull the anchor while you run, so the cooldown burns during the rotation instead of after it.

Ulted, play it like the Operator you didn't buy: long sightline, off-angle, first contact, one shot, then move.

Retakes are your worst phase; the kit buys almost nothing once the site is theirs. Swing with your team, trade like a rifle body, and don't be precious about it.

## Decisions that win or lose rounds

- **Anchor before peek. Always.** No exit placed, no aggressive angle taken. A peek without the anchor is a duel any duelist takes better than you.
- **Press the teleport on their commitment, not your damage.** The trigger is utility landing on you or a swing starting. Wait until you're tagged and the windup is where you die.
- **Bullet math.** Buy Headhunter bullets only when next round's full buy still fits. All eight on pistol round, three or four on a save, zero when it costs a rifle. And top up when you're rich; the stock carries over, so your next eco is decided now.
- **Ult: take the one reliable pick.** If a pick is there, shoot without hesitation; a guaranteed opener swings more rounds than a hypothetical triple. Don't hold it for a perfect round that never comes, don't pull it against a team that's saving, and don't stay for a second bullet while the trade is already swinging at you. Use the slow on purpose: the first man through the choke is worth more than a cleaner shot on the third, because his corpse slows everyone behind him.
- **The trap is a warning, not a doorstop.** At the site entrance it's shot in the first seconds, and a trap that dies to routine clearing tells you what the push already told you. Deep on a route behind cover it's a guarantee: this door is watched, and anyone through it fights you slowed.
- **Put slows inside your lane.** Anything crossing your sightline at half speed, from trap or ult kill, is target practice. Build for that overlap, and tell your team where the trap is, because it slows them just the same.

## Mistakes that actually cost rounds

Ranked by rounds burned:

1. **One Chamber, one spot.** Same angle, same anchor, every round. By round four they pre-fire the angle, then pre-fire the landing. The mirror mistake is playing him passive like a trap sentinel: parked in a corner, kit idle, an expensive rifle body.
2. **Peeking anchorless.** Or anchoring in the open, which is the same mistake on a delay: 50 HP, visible, gone for the round. They break it, then hunt you like a normal human.
3. **The panic teleport.** Pressed after you're hit, so you die in the windup. Pressed before any pressure, so space is handed over free. Or spent shuffling around, so it's on cooldown when the push actually comes.
4. **Trap in the doorway, or in your pocket.** At the obvious entrance it dies in seconds and half its information dies with it. Never placed at all is how one lurker kills three people who each assumed someone was watching.
5. **Headhunter bleed.** Spamming bodies, shooting smokes, running it as a primary on full buys. Every miss is a hundred credits.
6. **Ult greed, both kinds.** Sitting on it for five rounds waiting for the perfect moment, and staying scoped for a second kill while the refrag is mid-air.
7. **Re-peeking after the escape.** You teleported because the fight was lost; for the next half minute there is no second escape. Swinging back in throws away the round the teleport just saved.
